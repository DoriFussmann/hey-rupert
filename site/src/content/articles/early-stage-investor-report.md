## The Short Answer

Rupert's investor database covers more than 10,000 funds. **4,229 of them say they invest at Pre-Seed, Seed or Series A**, and five findings stand out for founders raising now:

1. **"Seed fund" and "Series A fund" are mostly the same funds.** 84% invest at both.
2. **Pre-seed is a real market.** 923 funds invest there, and they are younger and more focused on AI than the rest.
3. **AI leads, but most funds are specialists.** 37% list AI/ML; half list just one to three verticals.
4. **US capital is concentrated.** California, New York and Massachusetts hold 58% of US early-stage funds.
5. **Borders matter less than founders think.** About half of early-stage funds list a geography beyond their home country.

## 1. Early Stage Is Most of the Market

Of the 7,672 equity funds mapped in Rupert's database, **4,229 (55%) say they invest at Pre-Seed, Seed or Series A**. Most are venture capital firms, but not all:

| Early-stage funds by investor type | Funds |
|---|---|
| Venture capital firms | 3,836 |
| Private equity firms with an early-stage mandate | 276 |
| Organized angel groups | 80 |
| Family offices | 37 |
| **Total** | **4,229** |

Beyond these, the database holds **2,500 debt-only funds** (1,950 of them in the US, most of the rest in Europe), which are industry-agnostic and relevant when a raise includes debt. And among the early-stage equity funds, 91% invest in equity only.

**What it means for founders:** the investors who could fund your round are not the ten famous names. They are thousands of funds, most of them smaller and more focused.

> "There is an entire broad universe of investors out there for you. The job is to tap into it." — Dori Fussmann, founder of Rupert

---

## 2. "Seed Fund" and "Series A Fund" Are Mostly the Same Funds

| Stage pattern among early-stage funds | Share |
|---|---|
| Invest at both Seed and Series A | **84%** |
| Invest at Seed but not Series A | 3% |
| Invest at Series A but not Seed or Pre-Seed | 13% |
| Invest at Pre-Seed (usually alongside Seed and Series A) | 22% |
| Stop at Series A (no Series B or later listed) | 58% |
| Also invest at Series B | 42% |

The stage label tells you little. Almost every fund that does Seed also does Series A, and the majority stop there: they are early-stage specialists, not multi-stage giants with an early-stage desk. What separates them is whether they lead, how much they write and how early they're willing to go. Filter on those, not on the word "Seed" in a fund's description.

---

## 3. Pre-Seed Is a Real Market, Run by Newer Funds

923 funds say they invest at pre-seed: 22% of early-stage funds. They're a different crowd from the rest:

| | Pre-Seed funds | Seed funds | Series A funds |
|---|---|---|---|
| List AI/ML as a focus | **52%** | 39% | 36% |
| List fintech | **42%** | 33% | 31% |
| List healthtech | 25% | 21% | 20% |

And they're younger: **72% of pre-seed funds were founded since 2015**, against 48% of early-stage funds that don't do pre-seed. The earlier the stage, the newer the fund and the stronger its tilt toward the sectors with the most momentum.

> "If you think you're too early, you're wrong. A lot of funds do pre-seed. With a strong team, a real market, a clear product vision and conviction, you can earn those meetings." — Dori Fussmann

## 4. AI Leads, but Most Funds Are Specialists

| Most-listed verticals among early-stage funds | Share of funds |
|---|---|
| AI/ML | 37% |
| Fintech | 31% |
| SaaS | 30% |
| Healthtech | 20% |
| Life sciences | 19% |
| Cybersecurity | 13% |
| Infrastructure | 13% |
| Deep tech | 10% |
| Marketplaces | 10% |
| Climate tech | 9% |

Most funds are focused: **49% list one to three verticals**, 31% list four to nine, and only **2% list ten or more**. Another 18% list no vertical at all and behave as generalists. For founders, a fund that lists your vertical among a handful is a much stronger signal than one that lists everything.

## 5. US Capital Is Concentrated in Three States

About half of early-stage funds (2,166) are headquartered in the US, and 27% (1,122) in Europe. Within the US:

| US state | Early-stage funds |
|---|---|
| California | 756 |
| New York | 355 |
| Massachusetts | 152 |
| Texas | 101 |
| Florida | 83 |
| Illinois | 81 |
| Colorado | 52 |

California, New York and Massachusetts hold **58%** of US early-stage funds. Outside the US, the countries with the most early-stage funds are the UK (298), Germany (190), Canada (145), Israel (126), China (101), Japan (99) and India (90). For the Bay Area in detail, see our guide to [Silicon Valley VC firms](/articles/biggest-venture-capital-firms-in-silicon-valley/).

## 6. Borders Matter Less Than Founders Think

About **half of early-stage funds (49%)** list at least one geography beyond their home country, and so do **about one in three US early-stage funds (32%)**. A founder in Toronto, London or Tel Aviv with a real fit for a US fund's thesis is not ruled out by location, and the same is true in reverse.

> "Everybody wants to tap into good investments, regardless of where. You wouldn't pass on a good Japanese fund, and a smart Japanese fund won't pass on a good investment opportunity." — Dori Fussmann

Practical caveats still apply: some funds prefer companies they can reach easily, and US-led rounds often expect a US entity. Check where a fund's recent investments are based before writing it off, or assuming it's a fit.

---

## 7. A Young Industry

**53% of early-stage funds were founded since 2015, and 22% since 2020.** Only 11% predate 2000. Many of the investors most likely to back an early-stage company today are emerging managers on their first or second fund, with a strong incentive to find the next breakout company before the established firms do.

## What This Means for Founders

1. **Widen the list before you narrow it.** The universe is thousands of funds, not ten. Start broad, then filter hard for fit.
2. **Filter on behavior, not labels.** Stage words overlap; look at whether a fund leads, its check size and its recent deals.
3. **Don't wait to be "ready enough" for pre-seed.** Hundreds of funds invest there, and they skew toward newer managers looking for momentum.
4. **Favor specialists.** A fund that lists your vertical among a few is a better target than a generalist.
5. **Look beyond your own country,** while checking each fund's actual investing footprint.

## Methodology

- **Source:** Rupert's investor database as of November 2026: 7,672 equity funds from a licensed data provider, mapped to Rupert's stage, sector (29 industries, 34 verticals) and geography categories, plus 2,500 debt-only funds held separately. Funds added by Rupert through its own research are not included in these figures.
- **Self-described:** stages, sectors and geographies are what each fund says it invests in, not an analysis of its actual deals.
- **Early-stage** means the fund lists Pre-Seed, Seed or Series A among its stages. Percentages in sections 2 to 7 are shares of the 4,229 early-stage funds unless stated otherwise.
- **Geography** is by headquarters. "Beyond the home country" means the fund lists at least one country or region other than its home country.
- **No firm names** are published; all figures are aggregates.
- **How to cite:** "Rupert, Early-Stage Investor Report 2026, heyrupert.com."
