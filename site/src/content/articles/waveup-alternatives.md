## The Short Answer

The main Waveup alternatives are **managed outreach services** such as Rupert (outreach run for you, a setup fee and flat monthly fee, no success fee), Qubit Capital and Fundex; **network introductions** through Funden; and **fundraising software** such as OpenVC, Foundersuite and Metal, where you send the outreach yourself. Waveup bundles decks, models, outreach and closing support, so start by deciding which of those you actually need. If it's only the outreach, a managed outreach service is the closer fit.

## What Waveup Is, and Why Founders Look Elsewhere

Waveup is a London-based fundraising and growth advisory. It says it has helped more than 600 startups raise over $3 billion since 2014 (company-reported). Its range is wide: pitch decks, financial models, valuations, data rooms, investor targeting, outreach and closing support, plus work outside fundraising such as branding and CFO support, all sold through one subscription.

Its [outreach service](https://waveup.com/investor-targeting-and-outreach/) has three tiers. In **Foundations**, Waveup builds and scores the investor list and maps warm-introduction paths, and the founder runs the outreach. In **Managed**, Waveup also writes and sends the sequences and follows up, and the founder takes the meetings; it estimates four to six founder hours a week. **Full-Stack** adds term-sheet triage and closing support, scoped on a call.

That breadth is the reason to choose Waveup, and also the most common reason founders look for an alternative. Some already have a deck and a model and want only the outreach done. Some want outreach to go out from their own inbox. Some want a fee that has nothing to do with the raise. A managed outreach service such as Rupert covers that narrower job: it matches the company to investors, writes the outreach for the founder's approval, sends it from the founder's own inbox and runs the follow-up, for a setup fee and a flat monthly fee.

## Managed Outreach Alternatives

These replace Waveup's Managed tier: someone else researches, writes and sends.

### Rupert

**Type:** Managed investor outreach · **Fees:** setup fee, then a flat monthly fee, month to month · **Success fee:** none

Rupert runs a two-to-four-week setup: an intake on the company and the round, the narrative, expert feedback on the deck and materials, messaging, and investor matching against its own database of more than 10,000 funds. Matching looks at the funds that fit the stage, sector and geography, the investors behind your funded competitors, and funds that resemble them. Nothing goes live until the founder approves the list, the strategy and the messaging. Outreach then goes out from the founder's own inbox, with follow-up on a schedule, and every investor relationship stays the founder's. Rupert doesn't take a percentage of the round or equity; [the pricing page](/pricing/) explains the model.

**Best fit:** Pre-Seed to Series A founders who already have their materials, want outreach run for them in their own name, and want no fee tied to the raise.

### Qubit Capital

**Type:** Managed fundraising with AI investor matching · **Fees:** not published; an onboarding fee is mentioned · **Success fee:** a "hybrid" model is referenced without published terms

Qubit Capital combines an investor database it says covers more than 20,000 investors (company-reported) with a team that runs research, outreach, follow-up and tracking while the founder takes the meetings. Like Waveup, it also prepares decks, financial models and data rooms. Its published client and capital figures vary between pages, so ask for current numbers and for the fee terms in writing.

**Best fit:** founders who want a managed service plus help with materials, and are comfortable agreeing on fees after a call.

### Fundex (Fundraise as a Service)

**Type:** Fundraising software plus a managed service run by Fidelman &amp; Co · **Fees:** platform $100–$500/month; managed service from $6,000/month · **Success fee:** 3% on the managed service

Fundex sells a fundraising CRM and investor database, and a managed service in which Fidelman &amp; Co handles targeting, outreach campaigns, scheduling and feedback. Its [pricing page](https://www.usefundex.com/pricing) lists the managed service from $6,000 a month plus a 3% success fee, with no annual contract. It's a young company, founded in 2025, with little client proof on its site.

**Best fit:** founders who want a managed service and accept a success fee in exchange.

## Network Introductions

### Funden

**Type:** Assisted fundraising with a VC network · **Fees:** unclear · **Success fee:** none mentioned

Funden doesn't run cold outreach. It distributes the company's deal to its network of investment partners and makes double opt-in introductions, alongside coaching, deck review, financial models and data-room preparation. Published pricing is inconsistent across sources, and Funden asks founders to apply for plan details.

**Best fit:** founders who prefer curated introductions through a network to outbound email.

## Software Alternatives

If you'd rather run outreach yourself, these cost a fraction of an advisory retainer.

### OpenVC

**Type:** Free investor database and deck submission · **Fees:** free; Premium $99/month or $299/year · **Success fee:** none

OpenVC lists more than 16,000 early-stage investors (company-reported). Founders submit their deck to investors through the platform: one a day on the free plan, five a day on Premium, which also adds extra filters and an intro finder that scans Gmail and LinkedIn for warm paths. See our guide to [OpenVC alternatives](/articles/openvc-alternatives/) if you outgrow it.

**Best fit:** founders on a tight budget who want a free starting point.

### Foundersuite

**Type:** Fundraising CRM with an investor database · **Fees:** free Basic plan; paid plans about $69, $99 and $149 a month · **Success fee:** none

Foundersuite organizes your raise: an investor database, pipelines and email tools, with you doing the research, writing and sending.

**Best fit:** founders who want to run an organized raise themselves.

### Metal

**Type:** AI fundraising platform with a done-for-you tier · **Fees:** custom, not published · **Success fee:** none, per Metal

Metal offers investor discovery, warm-intro mapping through your Gmail and LinkedIn, pipeline management and AI-drafted outreach, with an Autopilot tier that adds round strategy and pipeline building. Pricing is set after a demo.

**Best fit:** founders who want an AI-heavy tool with some hands-on help.

## How to Choose

1. **List what you actually need.** If you have a deck, a model and a clear story, you don't need to pay for them again. Buy only the part you're missing.
2. **Ask who sends, and from whose inbox.** Emails from your own address read as you, and the replies and history stay with you. Emails from a provider's address or domain put the provider between you and the investor.
3. **Read the fee model closely.** A flat fee pays for the work. A success fee ties the provider's pay to the round, and a tail period can extend it after the engagement ends. Our guide to [capital raising fees](/articles/capital-raising-fees/) covers what's normal.
4. **Approve before anything goes out.** You should see the investor list, the messaging and the approach before your name is on a single email.
5. **Check the commitment.** Waveup says you can pause or stop at any time; ask every alternative the same question.
6. **Ask for proof you can check.** Company-reported totals of capital raised are hard to verify. Ask for references from founders at your stage and in your sector.

Waveup sells one subscription that can cover materials, outreach, closing and work beyond fundraising, and its outreach and pricing pages quote different monthly figures, so confirm the price for your scope. ## Key Takeaways

- Waveup is a broad advisory: decks, models, outreach and closing support in one subscription.
- If you only need outreach run for you, compare managed outreach services: Rupert, Qubit Capital and Fundex.
- Ask every provider who sends the emails, from whose inbox, and whether you approve them first.
- Fees vary widely: no success fee at Rupert, a success fee on a few engagements at Waveup, 3% at Fundex's managed service, unpublished at Qubit Capital.
- OpenVC, Foundersuite and Metal are the software options if you run the raise yourself.
