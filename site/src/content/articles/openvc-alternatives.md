## The Short Answer

OpenVC alternatives fall into two groups. **Fundraising software** such as Foundersuite, Metal and Fundex's platform gives you more tools or more sending, and you still do the work. **Managed outreach services** such as Rupert (outreach run for you from your own inbox, a flat monthly fee, no success fee), Waveup and Qubit Capital do the research, writing and follow-up for you. If OpenVC's limits are the problem, try other software; if your time is, use a managed service.

## What OpenVC Is, and Where It Stops

OpenVC is a free fundraising platform with a database of more than 16,000 early-stage investors (company-reported), a CRM and deck tracking. Its signature feature is deck submission: founders send their deck to investors through OpenVC, which screens each submission before it goes out. The free plan allows one submission a day. Premium, at $99 a month or $299 a year, allows five a day and adds extra investor filters and an intro finder that looks for warm paths through your Gmail and LinkedIn. OpenVC takes no success fee or equity.

It's an excellent free starting point. Founders tend to look for an alternative for one of two reasons. The first is volume: at five submissions a day, a list of 150 investors takes a month, and anything beyond that goes out from your own email anyway. The second is time: OpenVC gives you the list and the channel, but the research, the writing and the following up are still yours. That second gap is what a managed outreach service such as Rupert fills: it builds the investor list, writes the outreach for your approval, sends it from your own inbox and follows up on a schedule.

## Software Alternatives

These replace OpenVC with a different tool. You still run the raise.

### Foundersuite

**Type:** Fundraising CRM with an investor database · **Fees:** free Basic plan; paid plans about $69, $99 and $149 a month · **Success fee:** none

Foundersuite pairs a fundraising CRM with an investor database of more than 100,000 angels and VCs, by its own count, plus pipelines and email tools. Unlike OpenVC's screened deck submission, the outreach goes out from you; the paid tiers mainly differ in how many investors each pipeline can hold.

**Best fit:** founders who want to run a larger, organized raise themselves.

### Metal

**Type:** AI fundraising platform with a done-for-you tier · **Fees:** custom, not published · **Success fee:** none, per Metal

Metal offers investor discovery, warm-intro mapping through your Gmail and LinkedIn, pipeline management and AI-drafted outreach. Its Autopilot tier adds round strategy, collateral and pipeline building. Pricing is set after a demo.

**Best fit:** founders who want an AI-heavy tool and some hands-on help without fully outsourcing.

### Fundex (platform)

**Type:** Fundraising CRM and investor database · **Fees:** $100–$500 a month · **Success fee:** only on the managed service

Fundex's platform covers the CRM and database side; its managed service is described below. It's a young company, founded in 2025.

**Best fit:** founders who want a paid tool with the option to move to a managed service later.

## Managed Outreach Alternatives

These replace the work, not the tool: someone else researches, writes and sends.

### Rupert

**Type:** Managed investor outreach · **Fees:** setup fee, then a flat monthly fee, month to month · **Success fee:** none

Rupert runs a two-to-four-week setup: an intake on the company and the round, the narrative, expert feedback on the deck and materials, messaging, and investor matching against its own database of more than 10,000 funds. Matching looks at the funds that fit the stage, sector and geography, the investors behind your funded competitors, and funds that resemble them. Nothing goes live until the founder approves the list, the strategy and the messaging. Outreach then goes out from the founder's own inbox, with follow-up on a schedule, so it isn't capped at a handful a day and every investor relationship stays the founder's. [The pricing page](/pricing/) explains the model.

**Best fit:** Pre-Seed to Series A founders who have outgrown doing it themselves and want no fee tied to the raise.

### Waveup

**Type:** Fundraising and growth advisory with managed outreach · **Fees:** outreach page cites $5,000 and $10,000 monthly retainers; pricing page lists $7,500 and $12,500 a month · **Success fee:** none on advisory work; a few fundraising engagements combine a retainer with a success fee, stated upfront

Waveup is a London-based advisory that says it has helped more than 600 startups since 2014 (company-reported). Its [outreach service](https://waveup.com/investor-targeting-and-outreach/) has three tiers: Foundations (the investor list and warm-intro mapping, with the founder sending), Managed (Waveup also writes, sends and follows up) and Full-Stack (adds closing support). It also sells decks, models and data rooms. See our guide to [Waveup alternatives](/articles/waveup-alternatives/) for how it compares.

**Best fit:** founders who want materials, outreach and closing help from one firm, and have the budget for it.

### Qubit Capital

**Type:** Managed fundraising with AI investor matching · **Fees:** not published; an onboarding fee is mentioned · **Success fee:** a "hybrid" model is referenced without published terms

Qubit Capital combines an investor database it says covers more than 20,000 investors (company-reported) with a team that runs research, outreach, follow-up and tracking while the founder takes the meetings. It also prepares decks, financial models and data rooms. Ask for current client figures and fee terms in writing.

**Best fit:** founders who want a managed service plus help with materials, and are comfortable agreeing on fees after a call.

### Fundex (Fundraise as a Service)

**Type:** Managed service run by Fidelman &amp; Co · **Fees:** from $6,000 a month · **Success fee:** 3%

In Fundex's managed service, Fidelman &amp; Co handles targeting, outreach campaigns, scheduling and feedback. Its [pricing page](https://www.usefundex.com/pricing) lists it from $6,000 a month plus a 3% success fee, with no annual contract.

**Best fit:** founders who want a managed service and accept a success fee in exchange.

## How to Choose

1. **Name the limit you've hit.** If it's volume or features, switch software. If it's time, buy a managed service.
2. **Ask who sends, and from whose inbox.** Emails from your own address read as you, and the replies and history stay with you.
3. **Read the fee model closely.** Software has no success fee. Among managed services, a flat fee pays for the work, while a success fee ties the provider's pay to the round. Our guide to [capital raising fees](/articles/capital-raising-fees/) covers what's normal.
4. **Approve before anything goes out.** You should see the investor list and the messaging before your name is on a single email.
5. **Keep OpenVC if it works.** It's free, and many founders keep using its investor data alongside whatever they add.

## Key Takeaways

- OpenVC is a free investor database with screened deck submission, capped at one a day free and five on Premium.
- If the cap or the features are the problem, other software such as Foundersuite, Metal or Fundex's platform fits.
- If your time is the problem, a managed outreach service such as Rupert, Waveup or Qubit Capital does the research, writing and follow-up.
- Ask who sends the emails and from whose inbox; outreach from your own address keeps the relationships with you.
- Fees vary: no success fee on software or at Rupert, a few engagements at Waveup, 3% at Fundex's managed service, unpublished at Qubit Capital.
