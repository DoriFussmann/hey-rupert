## The Short Answer

Foundersuite is software. It gives you an investor database and a fundraising CRM, and you do the research, the writing and the sending. If that's the part you want to hand off, you need a **managed outreach service**, such as Rupert, Waveup, Qubit Capital or Funden, rather than another tool. If you're happy running outreach yourself and want a cheaper or different tool, the software alternatives are OpenVC and Metal.

Among managed services, three questions separate them more than anything else: **who sends the emails and from whose inbox**, **how they charge** (a flat fee or something tied to your raise), and **how much of the process they cover** beyond outreach.

## What Foundersuite Is, and What It Isn't

Foundersuite combines a fundraising CRM with an investor database of more than 100,000 angels and VCs, by its own count. Plans run from a free Basic tier to paid plans of about $69, $99 and $149 a month, with roughly 10% off annually, according to [an independent review](https://angelspartners.com/reviews/foundersuite/); the paid tiers mainly differ in how many investors each pipeline can hold. It charges no success fee.

It's a good tool for founders who want to run their own raise in an organized way. What it doesn't do is the work itself: choosing which investors fit, writing to each of them, and following up for weeks. That is what the managed options below sell, and it's why founders who outgrow Foundersuite often aren't looking for a better CRM at all.

## Managed Outreach Services

These are the real alternatives if what you want to stop doing is the outreach itself.

### Rupert

**Type:** Managed investor outreach · **Fees:** setup fee, then a flat monthly fee, month to month · **Success fee:** none

Rupert runs a two-to-four-week setup: an intake on the company, the narrative, expert feedback on the deck and materials, messaging, and investor matching from its own database (funds that fit the stage, sector and geography; the investors behind your funded competitors; and funds that resemble them). Nothing goes live until the founder approves it. Outreach then goes out from the founder's own inbox, with critical emails written for each investor and follow-up on a schedule, and every relationship stays the founder's. Rupert doesn't publish prices; [the pricing page](/pricing/) explains the model.

**Best fit:** Pre-Seed to Series A founders who want outreach run for them but want it to come from them, with no fee tied to the raise.

### Waveup

**Type:** Managed outreach and broader fundraising advisory · **Fees:** about $5,000/month (Foundations) and $10,000/month (Managed); Full-Stack individually scoped · **Success fee:** on a few engagements only

Waveup has been operating since 2014 and offers a wide range: pitch decks, financial models, valuation, investor targeting, outreach, data rooms and closing support. Its [outreach service](https://waveup.com/investor-targeting-and-outreach/) has three tiers. Foundations builds and scores the investor list and maps warm-introduction paths, and the founder runs the outreach. Managed adds the full sequence, sending, follow-up and meeting preparation. Full-Stack adds term-sheet triage and closing support. Its stack includes Clay, Apollo, Smartlead, Instantly, HubSpot and Attio, and it says it has more than 200 warm VC relationships (company-reported). Its pricing page says a success fee applies only to a few fundraising engagements and is stated upfront; subscriptions can be paused or stopped.

**Best fit:** founders who want one firm to help with materials, outreach and closing, and have the budget for it.

### Qubit Capital

**Type:** Managed fundraising with AI investor matching · **Fees:** not published; an onboarding fee is mentioned · **Success fee:** a "hybrid" model is referenced without published terms

Qubit Capital, founded in 2020, combines an investor database it says covers more than 20,000 investors (company-reported) with a team that runs research, outreach, follow-up and tracking, while the founder takes the meetings. It also prepares decks, financial models and data rooms, and offers term-sheet guidance. Its published client and capital figures vary between pages, so ask for current numbers.

**Best fit:** founders who want a managed service plus help with materials, and are comfortable agreeing on fees after a call.

### Funden

**Type:** Assisted fundraising with a VC network · **Fees:** unclear · **Success fee:** none mentioned

Funden takes a different route from cold outreach: it distributes the company's deal to its network of investment partners and makes double opt-in introductions, alongside coaching, deck review, financial models and data-room preparation. Published pricing is inconsistent across sources, and Funden asks founders to apply for plan details.

**Best fit:** founders who prefer curated introductions through a network over outbound outreach.

### Fundex (Fundraise as a Service)

**Type:** Fundraising software plus a managed service run by Fidelman &amp; Co · **Fees:** platform $100–$500/month; managed service from $6,000/month · **Success fee:** 3% on the managed service

Fundex sells a fundraising CRM and investor database, and a managed "Fundraise as a Service" in which Fidelman &amp; Co handles targeting, outreach campaigns, scheduling and feedback. Its [pricing page](https://www.usefundex.com/pricing) lists the managed service from $6,000 a month plus a 3% success fee, paid monthly, with no annual contract. It's a young company (founded 2025), with little client proof published on its site.

**Best fit:** founders who want a managed service and accept a success fee in exchange.

## Software Alternatives

If you're happy running outreach yourself, these tools compete with Foundersuite directly.

### OpenVC

**Type:** Free investor database and deck submission · **Fees:** free; Premium $99/month or $299/year · **Success fee:** none

OpenVC lists more than 10,000 investors (company-reported) and lets founders submit their deck to investors through the platform. According to its [help center](https://openvc.app/help), submissions go out from OpenVC's own address with replies going to the founder; free users can send one outreach a day and Premium users five, and Premium adds warm-intro finding.

**Best fit:** founders on a tight budget who want a free starting point.

### Metal

**Type:** AI fundraising platform with a done-for-you tier · **Fees:** custom, not published · **Success fee:** none, per Metal

Metal describes itself as an AI operating system for founders raising venture rounds: investor discovery, warm-intro mapping through your Gmail and LinkedIn, pipeline management and AI-drafted outreach. Its Autopilot tier adds round strategy, collateral and pipeline building. Pricing is set after a demo.

**Best fit:** founders who want a modern, AI-heavy tool and some hands-on help without fully outsourcing.

## Broader Advisory

### Quill (formerly Flowlie)

Quill is a managed finance back office (finance, HR, compliance) whose Capital service covers data rooms, cap tables, investor updates, round sizing, term-sheet comparison and debt. It charges a monthly retainer and says it never charges a success fee. Its pages don't say that it runs investor outreach. It's a fit for venture-backed companies that want their finance function and fundraising preparation run together, more than for founders looking for outreach.

## How to Choose

1. **Decide what you're outsourcing.** If it's organization, buy software. If it's the work of finding, writing to and following up with investors, buy a managed service.
2. **Ask who sends, and from whose inbox.** Emails from your own address read as you, and the replies and history stay with you. Emails from a provider's address or domain put the provider between you and the investor.
3. **Read the fee model closely.** A flat fee pays for the work. A success fee ties the provider's pay to the round, and a tail period can extend it after you stop working together. Our guide to [capital raising fees](/articles/capital-raising-fees/) covers what's normal.
4. **Check that you approve before anything goes out.** You should see the investor list, the messaging and the approach before your name is on a single email.
5. **Check the commitment.** Month to month is common among managed services; make sure you can stop without penalty.
6. **Ask for proof you can check.** Company-reported totals of capital raised are hard to verify. Ask for references from founders at your stage.

## Where Things Stand

Foundersuite remains a solid, low-cost tool for founders who run their own raise. The real choice for founders who want outreach done for them is among managed services, and in November 2026 they differ sharply: Rupert and Quill charge no success fee, Waveup charges one only on a few engagements, Fundex's managed service adds 3% of the raise, and several providers don't publish their fees at all. ## Key Takeaways

- Foundersuite is software; you still do the outreach. If you want that done for you, compare managed services, not other CRMs.
- The three questions that separate managed services: who sends and from whose inbox, how they charge, and how much they cover beyond outreach.
- Success fees vary widely: none at Rupert or Quill, a few engagements at Waveup, 3% at Fundex's managed service, unpublished at Qubit Capital.
- OpenVC is the main free software alternative; Metal is the AI-heavy option with a done-for-you tier.
- Approve the list and the messaging before anything goes out, and keep the relationships in your own name.
